Our Services

From everyday pain relief to sports injury rehabilitation, discover personalized physiotherapy services designed to restore movement, rebuild strength, and support your recovery.

OUR SERVICES

Consultations & Diagnostic Care

Personalized assessments and professional guidance to understand your condition and plan your recovery.

Online Consultation

Remote physiotherapy guidance and assessment.

What to Expect

  • Discussion of your symptoms, injury history, and recovery goals.
  • Review of previous medical reports or imaging when available.
  • Guided movement observations when appropriate.
  • Advice on rehabilitation exercises and possible next steps.

How to Prepare

  • Choose a quiet, well-lit location.
  • Prepare any previous medical reports.
  • Wear comfortable clothing for movement.
  • Ensure a stable internet connection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an online consultation replace an in-person examination?

Online consultations can provide useful guidance, but some conditions require a hands-on physical examination or further testing.

Orthopedic Consultation

Assessment of joint, muscle, and sports-related injuries.

What to Expect

  • Review of your symptoms, medical history, and physical activities.
  • Physical assessment of the affected muscles and joints.
  • Evaluation of mobility, strength, and functional movement.
  • Recommendations for rehabilitation or additional investigations when necessary.

How to Prepare

  • Bring previous imaging and medical reports.
  • Wear clothing that allows access to the affected area.
  • Prepare a list of current medications and allergies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an MRI before my consultation?

Not necessarily. The clinician will assess your condition and determine whether imaging is required.

Follow-up Consultation

Monitor recovery and adjust your rehabilitation plan.

What to Expect

  • Review of progress since your previous consultation.
  • Reassessment of pain, mobility, strength, and function.
  • Discussion of any difficulties during rehabilitation.
  • Updates to your treatment or exercise plan as needed.

How to Prepare

  • Bring any new test results or medical reports.
  • Make a note of changes in symptoms.
  • Prepare questions about your recovery progress.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I schedule follow-up appointments?

The frequency depends on your condition, rehabilitation progress, and treatment goals.

Nutrition Consultation

Nutritional guidance for recovery and physical performance.

What to Expect

  • Discussion of your eating habits and nutritional goals.
  • Review of training demands and recovery needs.
  • Assessment of dietary patterns and possible areas for improvement.
  • Individualized nutritional recommendations.

How to Prepare

  • Prepare a brief record of your usual meals.
  • Bring relevant medical or nutritional reports.
  • List supplements and medications you currently take.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is nutrition important during injury rehabilitation?

Appropriate nutrition can help support tissue repair, muscle maintenance, and general recovery.

Sports Psychology Session

Mental preparation and confidence during sports recovery.

What to Expect

  • Discussion of mental challenges related to injury or performance.
  • Strategies for managing rehabilitation-related stress.
  • Support with motivation and goal setting.
  • Confidence-building approaches for returning to sport.

How to Prepare

  • Think about the challenges affecting your confidence.
  • Identify personal recovery or performance goals.
  • Prepare any questions you would like to discuss.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can sports psychology help after an injury?

Yes. Psychological support can help athletes manage fear of reinjury, motivation, and confidence during their return to sport.

PHYSIOTHERAPY ASSESSMENTS

Objective Diagnosis & Functional Testing

Structured assessments to understand how you move, identify limitations, and guide your rehabilitation.

Movement Screening

Functional Movement Screening

What to Expect

  • Assessment of fundamental movement patterns.
  • Observation of mobility, stability, and movement control.
  • Identification of movement limitations or asymmetries.
  • A summary of findings to guide your rehabilitation.

How to Prepare

  • Wear comfortable athletic clothing and trainers.
  • Bring any relevant imaging or previous assessments.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is movement screening a treatment?

No. It is an assessment used to understand your movement patterns and help establish rehabilitation goals and priorities.

ROM Assessment

Range of Motion (ROM) Assessment

What to Expect

  • Measurement of joint mobility and flexibility.
  • Comparison of movement between the affected and unaffected sides.
  • Assessment of active and passive range of motion where appropriate.
  • Documentation of measurements to monitor progress.

How to Prepare

  • Wear clothing that allows easy access to the assessed joint.
  • Bring relevant medical reports if available.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is range of motion measured?

It helps identify movement restrictions and provides a baseline for tracking improvements during physiotherapy.

Balance & Movement Analysis

Balance and Functional Movement Assessment

What to Expect

  • Static and dynamic balance assessment.
  • Observation of coordination and postural control.
  • Functional movement tasks appropriate to your condition.
  • Identification of areas requiring rehabilitation or improvement.

How to Prepare

  • Wear comfortable sports clothing and suitable footwear.
  • Inform the physiotherapist about dizziness or previous falls.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is balance testing only for athletes?

No. Balance assessment can benefit athletes, people recovering from injuries, and individuals experiencing stability or mobility difficulties.

Foot & Gait Assessment

Foot Posture & Biomechanical Assessment

What to Expect

  • Evaluation of foot posture and alignment.
  • Observation of walking and movement mechanics.
  • Assessment of lower-limb control and loading patterns.
  • Recommendations based on identified movement findings.

How to Prepare

  • Bring your usual walking or sports shoes.
  • Wear clothing that allows observation of the lower limbs.
  • Bring relevant previous assessments if available.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can gait assessment help with sports injuries?

It can help identify movement and loading patterns that may be relevant to injury management and rehabilitation planning.

Outcome Scoring

Functional Recovery & Progress Evaluation

What to Expect

  • Use of structured questionnaires or functional measures.
  • Evaluation of symptoms, daily activities, and physical function.
  • Comparison of results throughout rehabilitation.
  • Review of progress toward individual recovery goals.

How to Prepare

  • Be ready to discuss your symptoms and functional limitations.
  • Bring previous assessment results when available.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is outcome scoring important?

It provides a consistent way to monitor changes in symptoms and function, helping clinicians evaluate rehabilitation progress.

DIAGNOSTIC IMAGING

Accurate Diagnosis & Imaging Guidance

Musculoskeletal ultrasound examinations to assess injuries, evaluate soft tissues, and support informed rehabilitation decisions.

Ultrasound Assessment

MSK Ultrasound Examination

What to Expect

  • Real-time ultrasound imaging of the affected region.
  • Examination of muscles, tendons, ligaments, and other accessible soft tissues.
  • Assessment of findings relevant to your symptoms or injury.
  • Discussion of results and possible next steps.

How to Prepare

  • Usually, no special preparation is required.
  • Wear clothing that allows access to the affected area.
  • Bring previous imaging and medical reports for comparison.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does musculoskeletal ultrasound use radiation?

No. Ultrasound uses sound waves rather than ionizing radiation and allows clinicians to examine certain structures in real time.

Dynamic Ultrasound Assessment

Dynamic Tendon & Ligament Assessment

What to Expect

  • Real-time ultrasound examination of selected tendons or ligaments.
  • Observation of structures during controlled movements.
  • Assessment of abnormalities that may become more apparent during movement.
  • Review of findings and recommendations for further management.

How to Prepare

  • No special preparation is usually needed.
  • Wear comfortable clothing that exposes the examined region.
  • Bring previous scans and reports if available.
  • Inform the clinician if certain movements cause significant pain.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a dynamic ultrasound different?

Unlike a static examination, dynamic ultrasound allows the clinician to observe certain tissues while you move, helping assess how they behave during functional activity.

INJURY CARE & SUPPORT

Casting, Splinting & Minor Office Procedures

Supportive injury management, immobilization, and assessment to help protect injured areas during recovery.

Casting & Bracing

Cast / Brace Application

What to Expect

  • Assessment of the injured area and its support needs.
  • Selection of an appropriate cast or brace when indicated.
  • Application and fitting by a qualified clinician.
  • Instructions for safe use and follow-up care.

How to Prepare

  • Bring previous X-rays, imaging, or medical reports.
  • Wear comfortable clothing that allows access to the injury.
  • Inform the clinician about skin irritation, allergies, or circulation problems.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a cast and a brace?

A cast generally provides more rigid immobilization, while a brace may offer adjustable or removable support depending on the injury and treatment plan.

Splinting

Protective Support & Immobilization

What to Expect

  • Assessment of the injured joint or limb.
  • Selection and fitting of a suitable splint.
  • Guidance on positioning and protecting the affected area.
  • Instructions for monitoring comfort, swelling, and circulation.

How to Prepare

  • Bring relevant medical reports or imaging.
  • Wear clothing that allows access to the injured area.
  • Explain any numbness, swelling, or changes in sensation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I remove my splint?

This depends on the type of splint and the injury. Follow your treating clinician's instructions before removing or adjusting it.

Wound Assessment

Wound Evaluation & Recovery Monitoring

What to Expect

  • Visual assessment of the wound and surrounding skin.
  • Evaluation of healing progress and possible complications.
  • Review of wound-care instructions and precautions.
  • Referral for additional medical care when necessary.

How to Prepare

  • Bring relevant medical or surgical records.
  • Share information about recent wound care and dressings.
  • Inform the clinician about fever, increasing pain, or unusual discharge.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should a wound be medically reviewed?

Increasing redness, swelling, warmth, pain, discharge, or fever can indicate a complication and should prompt medical assessment.

SPORTS PERFORMANCE

Performance & Objective Assessment

Objective strength, movement, and readiness testing to support rehabilitation and return-to-sport decisions.

Performance & Readiness Testing

Objective Performance Assessment (VALD)

What to Expect

  • Objective testing using VALD equipment, including force plates and dynamometry.
  • Measurement of strength, power, and left–right symmetry.
  • A report comparing your results with your baseline at each assessment checkpoint.

How to Prepare

  • Wear athletic clothing and trainers.
  • Avoid heavy training in the 24 hours before testing.
  • Stay hydrated and be prepared for light physical effort.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do the numbers tell me?

The measurements help quantify physical readiness by identifying strength deficits, asymmetries, and performance changes that may not be visible during a routine examination.

These results can help clinicians monitor rehabilitation progress and inform return-to-sport decisions alongside other clinical assessments.
